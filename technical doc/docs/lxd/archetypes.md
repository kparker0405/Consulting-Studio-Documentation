# Archetypes

Archetypes summarize patterns in participants’ decisions across the simulation. They provide a framework for reflection—not a ranking of teams or a direct measure of consulting ability.

The four strategic approaches represent defensible consulting philosophies with different strengths and tradeoffs. A fifth profile identifies a need for greater strategic coherence.

## Strategic Profiles

| Archetype | Variable anchors | Strength | Tradeoff to examine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise Growth Approach | Enterprise Impact + Strategic Decisiveness | Commercial drive, market vision, and timely action | Ensuring operational capacity and margins keep pace with growth |
| Community Anchor Approach | Critical Rigor + Stakeholder Trust | Risk management, local relationships, and sustainable value | Capturing additional growth without undermining the local foundation |
| Phased Innovation Approach | Critical Rigor + Resourcefulness | Evidence-backed planning, advisory support, and milestone-based investment | Maintaining execution momentum while validating assumptions |
| Yield & Value Maximization Approach | Enterprise Impact + Critical Rigor | Unit-economic analysis and pragmatic revenue optimization | Managing price sensitivity and delivering value to match increased spending |
| The Unaligned Agency | No clear anchor | Diagnostic prompt for examining decision coherence | Establishing clearer priorities, coordination, and proactive action |

## Midpoint Diagnostic

Before Level 3, participants receive a provisional profile based on their decisions to date.

The diagnostic identifies:

- The approach emerging from their choices
- A strength to preserve
- A tradeoff to monitor during disruption

It is not a directive to change strategy. Participants should evaluate whether their emerging approach remains appropriate.

## Final Debrief

After the final recommendation, the profile describes the strategic pattern across the completed engagement and identifies a practical implementation priority.

The debrief connects simulation choices to executive practice, such as protecting margins, coordinating a lean front office, maintaining agility, or managing customer price sensitivity.
